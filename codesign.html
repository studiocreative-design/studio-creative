Social Impact Research

Design for Social Impact was a project carried out at Monash University on collaboration with WonderLab (a co-design research lab invested in translating education research into high impact learning experiences) for the Department of Families, Fairness and Housing (DFFH) In Victoria.


Our objective was to conduct participatory design research activities on the topic of social inclusion followed by the creation of a theory of change, an evaluation plan and several speculative design concepts that could generate positive social impact.



How might we create a meaningful and lasting connection among people from different social groups to combat social exclusion and promote acceptance?

How might we foster mutual respect in contrast to ingrained negative attitudes and prejudice in order to achieve social inclusion?



When embarking on collecting data, we had settled on a two-stage approach: a co-design stage (Culture probe, Collage, Storytelling) & follow-up interviews.
I was the lead designer in creating 7 participatory research methods for the co-design stage, however, I had received invaluable insight and assistance from my fellow designers within the group.


After analysing the collected qualitative data, we came to the following conclusions:



Marking the difference between tolerance and true acceptance:
Explaining the difference between being mutually respectful & fully inclusive and only tolerating someone.

Mere exposure effect:
Considering exposure as first step in creating connection and targeting prejudice.

Engagement with public:
Children: through education (critical thinking) & interactive activities (games, sport)
Adults: through interactive activities (games, sport)

Our Theory of Change (TOC) reflected our final objective (mutual acceptance) and the key processes necessary to achieve it:



Shift in mindset >>> Will to connect >>> Meaningful connection >>> Reduced discrimination



Our speculative design concepts were targeting these stages, however, we tried to concentrate primarily on the most challenging first stage and how to achieve a shift in people's mindsets. Therefore, we selected games and game design as our main focus and showcased our board game during our final presentation to the client.


Welcome to Australia, a board game that encourages the players to share experiences about living in Australia, was targeted at people from different social and ethnic groups liivng often alongside in Victoria.


The idea behind this concept goes back to our qualitative research findings when we realised that many of our participants had experienced different forms of discrimination and they were ready to share their experiences with us, because they knew it would lead towards a deeper understanding.



Therefore, with this game, we tried to create a platform for sharing experiences and for improving awareness and understanding among the people within the society.



Our evaluation plan consisted of two stages: a short-term stage with a feedback survey; and a long-term stage with an interview survey. The feedback survey is handed out straight after playing the game and it measures satisfaction & raised awareness. The interview is conducted a couple of months after the game and it is aimed at creating a connection and a sense of belonging with the participants.





Other design concepts: Sync Vic (a mobile app for community engagement) and Come and Play Your Role (a card game for building empathy and unleashing creativity).


Welcome to Australia, Sync Vic & Come and Play Your Role were original design concepts created by SC and were further developed together within the group.
